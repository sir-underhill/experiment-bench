Experiments, not habits.

A habit is something you know is good and commit to indefinitely. An experiment is something you don't know will work for you — and the point is to find out. Failure isn't falling off the wagon; it's a data point.

This bench is set up from Mia's I Spent 3 Months Being Curious Instead Of Scrolling (experiment compilation), which builds on the experimental mindset framework. The goal: stop setting rigid goals based on predictions of what your best life looks like, and start running small, pre-planned experiments to discover it.

The mindset grid

Three mindsets hold us back; they're fluid, not fixed. The sweet spot is high ambition and high curiosity.

Escapismhigh curiosity, low ambition — endless rabbit holes, nothing built
Experimentalhigh curiosity, high ambition — open to uncertainty as opportunity
Cynicismlow both — low-effort negative vibes
Perfectionismhigh ambition, low curiosity — rigid goals, fear of the data

↑ ambition · curiosity →

The loop

  1. Observe your current situation — honestly, specifically.
  2. Ask a research question — “If I ___ for ___, will I ___?”
  3. Design a tiny experiment — smallest version, startable today, with resources you already have.
  4. Analyze and decide — keep it, revise the parameters, or drop it. All three are wins, because all three are data.

The rules that make it work

  • Commitment pact, not a habit pledge. “I will do X for exactly Y duration.” Actionable now, needs no extra resources, no other people — and it doesn't have to stick. You just have to finish the run.
  • Regularity over streaks. Mia's daily 15-minute crafting pact flopped as a schedule; reframed as project-based, it worked. A broken streak is not a broken experiment.
  • Intentions under goals. Her “buy a house” was really “financial security + a creative project.” Name the intention, and more experiment designs fit inside it.
  • Visual reminders (value tagging). Your brain filters ~11 million bits/second down to ~50; emotion and relevance decide what gets through. Put your focus areas where your eyes already go.
  • Write the prediction down. Then let the results surprise you.

Mia's first runs (for calibration)

Be my own algorithm kept

Pre-curate a content bank; only watch from the list. 2 weeks → rolled into quarterly. Result: watched less, enjoyed more, journaling bloomed. Not life-changing, genuinely useful.

Commonplace book, round two running

3-month pact. Round one failed a year earlier — “maybe it's just not for you right now.” Foundation changed; the urge finally showed up.

Crafting for cognition revised

Daily 15-min pact flopped; cognitive benefit (flow-state reset) was real. Round two: project-based, two crafts, regular over strict.

12 pieces of clothing/year running

Track every purchase: material, cost, fit-in-wardrobe, likes/dislikes. Tracking killed impulse buys — “carts left abandoned.”
